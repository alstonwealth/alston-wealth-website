Last edition was about when to claim Social Security, and why for a married couple it's really a decision about the survivor. This edition is about the part of your money that's supposed to be the safe part.

For decades the rule was simple: more bonds as you get older. Then 2022 happened, and a lot of retirees found out that rule has a weak spot. I made a short video on it, and the full write-up is on our site.

Why bonds didn't protect people in 2022

The real reason to own bonds in retirement isn't the interest. It's having something that holds steady when stocks fall, so you never have to sell stocks at the bottom to pay your bills.

But when interest rates go up, the value of existing bonds goes down. If you own a single bond, you can hold it to the end and still get your money back. Most people don't own single bonds, though. They own bond funds, and a bond fund never matures. When rates rise, it just goes down.

In 2022 rates rose faster than they had in decades. The broad U.S. bond market lost about 13%, its worst year on record, while stocks fell about 18%. Both halves of the "safe" plan fell together.

What an annuity does differently

A fixed index annuity is an insurance contract, not an investment. You're credited interest based on a market index, with a floor that's usually zero. A bad market year means you earn nothing that year, not a loss. And rising interest rates don't lower your account value.

But it costs you something:

  • Your money is tied up. Surrender periods of seven to ten years are common, with a penalty for taking out more than the free amount.
  • Extra features can have yearly fees, especially lifetime income features.
  • The guarantee is only as strong as the insurance company behind it.

Bonds win on getting to your money quickly, seeing exactly what you own, and gaining value when rates fall.

So which one?

Usually some of both, each sized to the job it has to do. Money you'll need in the next two or three years belongs in cash. Money that has to pay you an income you can't outlive is a different job from money that needs to stay flexible. The annuity can cover the floor; bonds give you flexibility and dry powder.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did bonds lose money in 2022?
Interest rates rose quickly. When rates go up, older bonds paying lower rates are worth less, so bond prices and bond funds fall. The broad U.S. bond market lost about 13% that year.

Can a fixed index annuity lose money when the market drops?
The market drop itself doesn't reduce your account value, because the floor is usually zero. But fees for extra features and surrender charges for early withdrawals can reduce what you get back.

Should I move my bonds into an annuity?
Not automatically. It depends on what that money needs to do. Money for the next few years usually belongs in cash, and some money should stay flexible. An annuity fits best when the job is a steady income floor you can't outlive.
