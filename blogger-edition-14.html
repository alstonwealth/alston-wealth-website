Most people think about Social Security as one person's decision: claim early, claim at full age or wait until 70. For a married couple, it's really two decisions, and the higher earner's choice ends up deciding what the survivor lives on. The full write-up with the break-even math is on our site. What happens when one spouse dies

The survivor doesn't keep both checks. The smaller one stops, and the survivor keeps whichever check is bigger. Say one spouse gets $3,100 a month and the other gets $1,750. After the first death, the household goes from $4,850 a month to $3,100.

That's why the higher earner's claiming age matters so much. If the higher earner claims at 62 instead of waiting, the survivor's check can be hundreds of dollars a month smaller, for the rest of their life. And the survivor often lives 10 or 20 years after the first spouse is gone.

A common approach for couples

  1. The lower earner claims earlier, sometimes at 62, sometimes at full retirement age. Their spousal benefit doesn't grow after full retirement age, so waiting past 67 usually gains them nothing.
  2. The higher earner waits as long as the budget comfortably allows, often to 70. Every year past 67 adds 8% to the check, for life.
  3. Savings cover the gap in the meantime, which can also be a chance to draw from accounts you haven't paid tax on while your tax brackets are low.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a surviving spouse get both Social Security checks?
No. The survivor keeps the larger of the two checks, and the smaller one stops.

How much is the spousal benefit?
Up to half of the other spouse's full-retirement-age amount, if that's more than your own benefit. It's reduced if you claim before your full retirement age.

What's the break-even age for waiting?
Comparing 62 with 67, the person who waited catches up in total dollars around age 79. Comparing 62 with 70, it's around 80 or 81. For a couple, the better question is whether either of you will live past that age.

Can I claim early while I'm still working?
You can, but if you earn over the yearly limit before your full retirement age, Social Security holds back part of your check until you reach full retirement age.
